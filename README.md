# Foundry

Foundry is a real-time Three.js simulation substrate built around a deformable physical world and the systems that act upon it.

The current executable combines an octagonal volumetric terrain field with matter, creatures, deployable machinery, payloads, logistics, water, settlement-scale systems, and direct terrain manipulation. It is less a finished game than a place for discovering what becomes possible when those systems share one world.

## Enter the Foundry

**[Open the live executable](https://bonoj.github.io/Foundry/)**

Foundry is meant to be encountered directly. Orbit the world, raise or carve terrain, deploy probes, call down payloads, and watch the resulting systems interact.

The compact controls at the bottom expose the ordinary play surface. The **FOUNDRY** panel exposes the larger experimental workbench when deeper inspection is useful.

## What it is exploring

Foundry grew out of World Lab as a particularly fertile substrate for experiments involving:

- deformable terrain as world state rather than scenery
- physical matter and terrain modification
- autonomous and deployable actors
- logistics and construction
- settlement and infrastructure
- interactions between systems operating at different scales

Some of those mechanisms are mature experiments; others remain exposed machinery. Their coexistence is intentional: Foundry is still an active place of investigation.

## Current authority

The executable at `index.html` is the ground truth for what Foundry can presently do.

This README is a provisional introduction, not a complete semantic specification. Foundry has not yet received the full semantic reconciliation applied to some later World Lab descendants. Where prose and executable behavior differ, trust the executable.

## Lineage

Foundry was lifted from the larger World Lab ecology after its terrain, matter, deployment, and world-system experiments developed enough identity to stand on their own.

The inherited implementation still contains useful experimental archaeology. That history is evidence of how the substrate developed; it is not a requirement that future implementations preserve the same machinery.

Foundry is expected to keep changing.
